Operation contracts ​

This page describes the Julia interface. An operation in tenferro-rs does not automatically imply the same operation is exposed by Tenferro.jl.

Types and devices ​

AutoTenferro() and AutoTenferroTraced() accept CPU arrays of Float32, Float64, ComplexF32, and ComplexF64. Integer and Bool arrays are storage and forward-computation types, not differentiable inputs. GPU construction, device transfer, and GPU execution are not exposed by this Julia package. Converting a GPU array through the AbstractArray constructor would copy to the host; it does not select a GPU backend.

TypedTensor provides storage and Julia's AbstractArray fallback operations. Those fallback operations are not recorded for Tenferro AD. Use EagerTensor or TracedTensor for the Rust operation and AD surfaces described here.

Arithmetic and broadcasting ​

A + B and A - B require equal shapes. A * B is matrix multiplication (or a matrix–vector product), whereas A .* B is elementwise multiplication. Dot operations support Julia's leading-axis broadcast convention: missing trailing dimensions have extent one. The Julia adapter explicitly expands operands before calling Rust, so Rust's own implicit broadcast convention is not used. Cotangents of expanded axes are summed back to each input's shape.

Binary dot operations are +, -, *, /, ^, rem (also .%), min, and max. Scalars and constant Julia arrays can appear on either side. Mixed floating-point and complex operands use Julia's promote_type; cotangents are converted back to each active input's type. Unsupported promoted types are rejected. Tensors in one operation must share a context.

+, *, max and min broadcast over more than two arguments as Julia's left fold, for example max.(x, y, 0), and muladd.(a, b, c) and fma.(a, b, c) are a .* b .+ c. Partially applied functions with a scalar, such as (>(0)).(x) or Base.Fix1(-, 1).(x), broadcast as the binary operation. zero.(x) and one.(x) are constant tensors. similar throws an ArgumentError: tensors are immutable.

Same-type Int32/Int64 tensors support forward +, -, elementwise *, rem, min, max, abs, and sign. Elementwise / converts integer and Boolean operands to Float64, matching Julia rather than Rust's integer division. Mixed integer tensor types and integer type promotion are not a supported general interface. Boolean arithmetic is otherwise rejected by Rust.

clamp.(x, lo, hi) is composed as min.(max.(x, lo), hi) and allows broadcast bounds. abs., sign., conj., and unary - are elementwise operations. Complex abs returns the corresponding real element type. Ordering operations (min, max, clamp, minimum, maximum) are restricted to real values.

Derivatives at quotient discontinuities of rem, zero of abs/sign, power branch points, or equal operands of min/max are not classical derivatives. rem uses derivatives 1 and -div(x,y) away from its discontinuities; its divisor must be nonzero. Complex sign uses the real derivative of z / abs(z) away from zero. Do not use finite differences across these discontinuities as a gradient reference.

Element-type casts and constants ​

S.(x) converts a tensor to any tensor element type S (Float32, Float64, ComplexF32, ComplexF64, Int32, Int64, Bool) with the tenferro-rs cast, for example Float32.(x) of a Float64 tensor, ComplexF64.(x) of a real tensor, or Float32.(x .> 0) of a mask; float.(x) casts to the floating-point type of eltype(x). convert(EagerTensor{S}, x) (and likewise for TracedTensor) is the same conversion and returns x itself when it already has the requested type. Casts between floating-point and complex types are differentiable: cotangents are cast back to the input type. Casts to or from integer and Bool types carry no derivative.

Conversions follow Julia's S(v) for each element:

  • Conversions that are exact for every value of the source type always succeed: to floating-point and complex types from real types (rounding to nearest, as Float32(v) does), from Bool, and from Int32 to Int64.

  • Conversions that depend on the values (a floating-point or complex value to an integer type, Int64 to Int32, anything but Bool to Bool, and complex to real) check every element of an eager tensor and throw Julia's InexactError when one is not exactly representable: Int64.(x) throws for 2.7, NaN or 2.0^63, Bool.(x) for anything but 0 and 1, Int32.(x) for 2^40, and Float64.(z) for a nonzero imaginary part. The check costs one read of the tensor back to the host.

  • On a TracedTensor the values are only computed when the graph is evaluated, so these value-dependent conversions throw an ArgumentError instead. Use an explicit rounding (trunc.(Int64, x), round.(Int64, x), floor.(Int64, x) or ceil.(Int64, x)), x .!= 0 for Bool, x .% Int32 to wrap integers, or real.(z) and imag.(z) for complex tensors.

trunc.(x), floor.(x), ceil.(x) and round.(x) (ties to even, as in Julia; round.(x, mode) also accepts RoundNearestTiesAway, RoundNearestTiesUp, RoundToZero, RoundFromZero, RoundUp and RoundDown) round real floating-point tensors and keep their element type, NaN, infinities and the sign of zero. Their derivative is zero (for magnitudes of 2^62 and above, where every float is already an integer, it is zero up to rounding in the accumulated cotangent). Integer tensors round to themselves. They are composed from casts, comparisons and selections, so they cost more than one elementwise operation.

trunc.(S, x), floor.(S, x), ceil.(S, x), round.(S, x) and round.(S, x, mode) for S in Int32, Int64 and Bool round and then convert. On an eager tensor a value whose rounding is out of range for S, or is NaN or infinite, throws the same InexactError as Julia. On a traced tensor the backend cast reports such values as an error when the graph is evaluated; a Bool target is rejected with an ArgumentError. An integer tensor converts exactly, as S.(x) does. x .% S for an integer tensor x and S in Int32 or Int64 wraps like Julia's rem(v, S) on both kinds of tensor. Mixed-type arithmetic promotes with the raw cast, which only widens.

Tenferro.context(x) returns the EagerContext or TracedContext that owns x, and Tenferro.constant(x, array) copies a Julia array or number into it as a constant, without derivatives, keeping its element type. Inside a function differentiated by AutoTenferro() or AutoTenferroTraced(), this is how to create tensors that combine with the traced input.

Comparisons and selection ​

.==, .!=, .<, .<=, .> and .>= compare tensors, scalars and constant Julia arrays with the broadcast and promotion rules above and return Bool tensors. Comparisons are not differentiable: no cotangent flows through them. .! negates a Bool tensor. ifelse.(mask, a, b) selects elementwise; the mask may be a Bool tensor, a Bool array or a Bool, and the branches may be tensors, scalars or constant arrays of a common promoted type. Cotangents flow into the selected branch only. abs2.(x) and inv.(x) are composed from the supported arithmetic operations.

Elementwise mathematical functions ​

The eight supported broadcast functions call the corresponding Rust operations:

JuliaDerivative away from poles and branch cuts
exp.(x), expm1.(x)exp.(x)
log.(x)1 ./ x
log1p.(x)1 ./ (1 .+ x)
sqrt.(x)1 ./ (2 .* sqrt.(x))
sin.(x)cos.(x)
cos.(x)-sin.(x)
tanh.(x)1 .- tanh.(x).^2

Complex VJPs conjugate the analytic derivative. Tests compare both precisions and complex types against Julia values, analytic derivatives, and directional finite differences away from singularities. sqrt at zero, log at zero, and log1p at minus one have singular derivatives. Complex branch cuts and poles are excluded from differentiability claims. Real input is not implicitly promoted to complex to extend a function's domain.

Real negative arguments of log and sqrt, and arguments below minus one of log1p, currently produce Rust's IEEE NaN, whereas Julia Base throws a DomainError. log(0) and log1p(-1) produce -Inf. Integer and Boolean inputs to these eight mathematical functions are rejected rather than implicitly converted to floating-point; explicitly convert the input first.

exp(A) is a matrix function and is not provided by these elementwise methods. Loading SpecialFunctions.jl enables erf.(x) for real tensors through a package extension.

Broadcast fallback ​

Only the functions listed in this guide are lowered to tenferro-rs. Broadcasting any other function over a tensor, including an anonymous function or a closure, throws an ArgumentError that names the function; write its body as separate dotted operations. (f ∘ g).(x...) is expanded to f.(g.(x...)), and identity.(x) returns x. An EagerScalar broadcasts with tensors, numbers and constant arrays.

Neural-network operations ​

These operations are available after using NNlib (package extension TenferroNNlibExt), for eager and traced tensors, and are differentiable by both backends.

NNlib functionImplementation and derivative at kinks
sigmoid/σ, sigmoid_fast, softplus, swish, gelu/gelu_tanh, gelu_erftenferro-rs activation kernels (overflow-free sigmoid and softplus)
relunative kernel x > 0 ? x : 0 (a tenferro-rs extension, with derivative x > 0: 0 at the kink)

The activations accept Float32 and Float64 tensors, and eager scalars. leakyrelu, elu, celu, softshrink and trelu also accept their parameter as a constant real number, as in leakyrelu.(x, 0.2). rrelu (random) is not provided. NNlib.fast_act(f, x) returns f for tensors. Unlike NNlib's tanh_fast and sigmoid_fast, the tensor versions are not approximations, so Float32 results can differ from NNlib's in the last few bits.

softmax(x; dims=1), logsoftmax(x; dims=1) and logsumexp(x; dims=:) accept an integer, a tuple of dimensions or :. For a single dimension, softmax and logsoftmax use the tenferro-rs kernels; otherwise they are composed from maximum, exp, sum and log, shifted by the maximum along dims.

batched_mul(A, B) (A ⊠ B) multiplies the matrices A[:, :, k] and B[:, :, k] through tenferro-rs dot_general with a batch dimension. It follows NNlib: a 3-D operand with batch size one is reused for every batch, a matrix operand multiplies every batch, and higher-rank operands with equal trailing dimensions are reshaped to one batch dimension. Either operand may be a constant Julia array. batched_transpose and batched_adjoint return permuted (and, for complex tensors, conjugated) tensors, not lazy wrappers.

pad_zeros(x, pad; dims=:) and pad_constant(x, pad, val=0; dims=:) follow NNlib's padding arguments: an integer, one amount per padded dimension, a (low, high) amount pair per padded dimension, or a tuple of (low, high) pairs. They use tenferro-rs pad; a nonzero val adds a constant frame. Negative padding (cropping) is not supported.

Convolution, pooling and upsampling ​

tenferro-rs has no convolution or reduce_window primitive. For Float32 and Float64 tensors, Tenferro's bridge adds native window kernels as a tenferro-rs extension family: im2col (copy every window into a column matrix) and its transpose col2im, window sums and their transpose, and first-maximum pooling with its tangent and cotangent maps. Every derivative of a member is a member again, so pushforwards and pullbacks of any order run on these kernels, eagerly and traced. Other element types (complex tensors) compose the same operations from pad, slice, concatenate, reduction and select kernels. All of them follow NNlib's WHCN layout, (spatial..., channels, batch) with one to three spatial dimensions, and NNlib's argument conventions.

PoolDims, DenseConvDims and DepthwiseConvDims accept tensors in place of arrays. Either operand of conv, depthwiseconv, ∇conv_data and ∇conv_filter may be a constant Julia array. maximum and minimum accept init (Lux's GlobalMaxPool calls maximum(x; dims, init=-Inf)), so Lux's global and adaptive pooling layers work. Anti-aliased bilinear upsampling, upsample_bicubic and complex inputs are not provided.

NNlib.gather(src, idx) (and gather(src, I, J, ...)) accepts integers, integer tuples and CartesianIndexes as indices: one axis gather of src with the indexed trailing dimensions flattened. NNlib.scatter(op, src, idx; init, dstsize) supports op in +, - and Statistics.mean, built on the additive tenferro-rs scatter; repeated indices accumulate. Other operators (max, min, *, /) throw an ArgumentError. Indices are checked on the host; out-of-range indices throw a BoundsError instead of being clamped.

Dense layers and cross-entropy ​

Tenferro.dense(σ, W, x, b=nothing) computes σ.(W * x .+ b) for a weight matrix, an input vector or matrix and an optional bias vector, and Tenferro.logitcrossentropy(ŷ, y; agg=mean) computes the softmax cross-entropy of logits with classes along the first dimension (Lux.CrossEntropyLoss(; logits=Val(true)) without label smoothing). Both are part of Tenferro itself and need no package extension; NNlib.relu is recognized as a native dense activation once NNlib is loaded.

FunctionImplementation
dense with identity or relu, Float32/Float64 operandsone native kernel: a GEMM (Julia's BLAS) followed by one pass for the bias and the activation; its tangent and cotangent maps are native kernels of the same family, so derivatives of any order stay native
dense with any other activation or element typeW * x (dot_general), a bias broadcast and σ.(...)

The cross-entropy's first derivatives (gradients, pullbacks, pushforwards) are native for both logits and targets. Second derivatives through the softmax, such as a Hessian with respect to the logits, are not implemented and throw an error. Other element types and aggregations throw an ArgumentError.

Reductions ​

sum, prod, maximum, minimum, and Statistics.mean accept dims; prod, maximum and minimum also accept init, combined with the result by one more *, max or min. Statistics.var and Statistics.std accept dims, corrected and a precomputed mean; they are composed from mean, abs2 and sum. sum(f, x), prod(f, x), maximum(f, x), minimum(f, x) and Statistics.mean(f, x) reduce f.(x), with the same keywords; f must be a supported elementwise function such as abs2. Without dims, they reduce all elements to a scalar. With dims, Julia dimensions are converted from one-based to zero-based Rust axes; reduced dimensions are restored with extent one. Tuples of axes may be unordered. Duplicate or nonpositive axes are rejected; axes beyond the input rank are ignored. An empty tuple of axes returns the input unchanged.

Product derivatives account for zero factors: one zero receives the product of the other factors, and two or more zeros give zero derivatives. Tied reduction extrema divide the cotangent equally among the tied elements. This is a selected subgradient, not differentiability at a tie. It differs from ChainRules' extrema rules, which route the seed to the single index selected by findmin/findmax. For example, the gradient of minimum([1,1,2]) here is [0.5,0.5,0], whereas ChainRules selects [1,0,0]. Product zero-factor handling agrees with the ChainRules product rule. These comparisons do not install or invoke ChainRules during Tenferro AD. Complex sum, prod, and mean are supported; complex extrema are not.

On zero-length reduced axes, the Julia wrapper supplies the sum/product identities, mean returns NaN, and maximum/minimum throw as in Julia. The backend identity extension is tracked in tenferro-rs#2453. A zero-length non-reduced dimension gives an empty output with the expected shape. An empty input is distinct from an empty tuple of reduction axes. The dtype and AD guarantees in this section apply to floating/complex types; integer widening (for example Julia's sum(::Vector{Int32})) is not a supported Julia-compatible contract.

Bool tensors reduce like Julia's: sum(mask) and count(mask) (also count(f, x)) return Int64 counts, mean(mask) and the mean of an integer tensor are Float64, and any(mask), all(mask), any(f, x) and all(f, x) return Bool, all with optional dims. Without dims, any and all return a zero-dimensional Bool tensor (an eager scalar or a zero-dimensional traced tensor), not a Julia Bool, so convert it with only(any(mask)) or Bool(any(mask)) before using it in if or &&.

Shapes, indexing, and ownership ​

reshape follows Julia column-major element order. permutedims accepts a permutation of one-based axes. A vector's transpose or adjoint has shape (1,n); a matrix swaps its two axes. adjoint also conjugates values and its pullback conjugates the seed; transpose does not conjugate. Higher-rank transpose is rejected; use permutedims instead.

reverse accepts one dimension or a tuple of dimensions. cat accepts one positive dimension; lower-rank operands get trailing singleton dimensions, so cat(a, b; dims=3) of matrices or hcat of vectors works as in Julia. Every other axis must have equal extents; floating-point/complex types are promoted. vcat and hcat of tensors are cat along dimensions 1 and 2. Constant Julia arrays may be mixed with tensors in cat, vcat and hcat (the first tensor must be among the first three arguments), for example hcat(x, ones(3, 1)). vec(x) flattens to a vector, and repeat(x, counts...) and repeat(x; inner, outer) follow Base with positive counts, as a reshape and broadcast_to whose cotangents sum over the repeated entries. Block concatenation tuples are not exposed. dropdims, selectdim, and eachslice (returning a Julia array of tensor slices, with drop) follow Base.

Indexing supports one integer, nonempty positive-step integer range, :, nonempty integer vector, or Boolean mask per dimension, for example W[:, [1, 3, 3]]. Integer dimensions are dropped. Integer vectors gather along their dimension; repeated indices accumulate their cotangents. Empty ranges and negative steps are explicitly rejected; A[:] flattens. Linear indexing of matrices, Cartesian index arrays, view, and setindex! are not exposed. tril, triu, and nonempty diag use Julia's signed diagonal offsets.

Input construction and Array(tensor) each copy data. Raw Julia pointers are used only during the FFI call under GC.@preserve; Rust copies input buffers before returning. A tensor retains its shared context, so the context variable need not remain in the caller's scope. Rust may share immutable storage for shape/view operations, but no mutable Julia alias or write-through view is returned. Mutating an input Julia array or a materialized result does not modify a tensor. Explicitly closing a context invalidates every tensor that shares it. Context accesses are serialized by a Julia lock.

Linear algebra ​

The Julia decomposition results are lightweight Tenferro containers. They are not subtypes of Julia's standard factorization types and do not provide all their methods or keyword options. For an m × n matrix and k = min(m,n):

OperationResult contract
svd(A)Thin U: (m,k), real S: (k,), Vt: (k,n); A ≈ U * Diagonal(S) * Vt; .V is Vt'; iteration gives U,S,V
qr(A)Unpivoted thin Q: (m,k), R: (k,n); A ≈ Q*R
lu(A)P: (m,m), L: (m,k), U: (k,n); P*A ≈ L*U; no .p or Julia pivot-vector contract
cholesky(A)Lower factor .L, conjugate transpose .U; A ≈ L*U; checks Hermitian input
eigen(A).values, .vectors; general eigenvalues and eigenvectors are complex even for real input
eigen(Hermitian(A,uplo))Real eigenvalues; uses only the selected triangle, with real diagonal
A \ BSquare solve, or least squares for rectangular A; accepts vector or matrix right-hand side
LowerTriangular(A) \ B, UpperTriangular(A) \ BUses the selected triangle
det, inv, pinv, normScalar determinant, inverse, pseudoinverse, and Euclidean/Frobenius norm

Eigenvalues use Julia-compatible sorting by default; sortby=nothing retains backend order. Decomposition signs and complex phases are not fixed to match Julia; compare reconstructions, invariant quantities, or consistently aligned factors. Batch dimensions, pivoted QR, full SVD, configurable pseudoinverse tolerances, and general norm orders are not exposed. Full SVD is explicitly rejected because Rust does not supply its AD rule.

The adapter propagates numerical failures from Rust. Singular solves/inverses, nonpositive Cholesky inputs, nonconvergence, changing rank, repeated singular or eigenvalues, zero norm, and pivot changes may fail or have undefined or ill-conditioned derivatives. These are not covered by smooth-gradient claims. The default pinv rank threshold belongs to Rust; it is not promised to equal Julia's default threshold at a numerical rank boundary.
